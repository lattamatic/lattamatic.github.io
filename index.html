L'ORÉAL · GENAI · CONTENT WORKFLOW

AI-generated SEO metadata, built to scale

Getting search metadata written for every page, in every market, without asking local teams to become SEO writers.

Problem
Page metadata is critical for search, and it was the part nobody filled in. Local teams focused on what visitors see. Many could not afford a local SEO agency and did not know how to write good metadata themselves. On top of that, the group has its own rules for metadata, such as length limits, which made writing it correctly even harder.
My role
I worked with the innovation team on the plan to bring in a third-party GenAI solution. My part was the product question: where in the content workflow should generation happen to get the best result, and how do we design that integration so it can scale across hundreds of group websites instead of being a one-brand tool.
Options weighed
  • Local SEO agencies. Good quality, but out of reach for many markets.
  • Scripted templates such as "Brand + Page title + Product category". Cheap and consistent, but blind to what the page actually says.
  • GenAI, the choice. It writes from the page's real context and still follows the rules set by the global SEO team.
Concerns I had to answer
Brands and local teams had to be brought on board, and their worries were specific: would it respect the brand's tone, and would it avoid black-listed wording?
How we knew it was good enough
We did not judge by impression. Each pilot market had a golden dataset to evaluate the generated metadata against before anything scaled.
Rollout
One brand and three pilot markets first, which took about two months. After that, onboarding a new market took less than a week, and generating metadata for new or updated pages took less than a day.
Outcome
Live across 50+ languages, with an average gain of two search ranking positions. The work won Best Content Management Modernisation from Sitecore (2023) and was a global finalist at L'Oréal's Digital Acceleration Summit (2022).

CASE 01 · DATA PRODUCT · OPEN BETA

Ville Idéale

A comparison engine for choosing where to live in Île-de-France: all 1,265 communes, down to neighbourhood level, on official open data.

Problem
Someone moving to the Paris region has to weigh rent, schools, safety, services and commute across 1,265 communes using scattered sources. Review sites are subjective, property portals are built for listings, and nothing answers the question below commune level.
Who it is for
I mapped five personas and chose to serve two: the arriving outsider with no mental map of the region, and the Paris-leaver with kids. Locals are treated as a data QA source, not as the target.
Decisions that shaped it
  • Rank, don't grade. Every criterion is a percentile across all communes ("top 12% for schools"). There is no objective 7.4/10 town, but a rank is defensible and checkable.
  • Neighbourhood granularity (INSEE IRIS) as the differentiator. The closest competitor states it stops at commune level.
  • User-adjustable weights are core, because the right ranking depends on the person.
  • Merged métro into commute scoring after spotting inner-ring towns ranked wrongly. It corrected 140 communes.
What I rejected
Display ads (they break the "open data, no ads" trust position), a département layer (it hides the variance the product exists to show), and affiliate links until there is traffic and trust.
Where it stands
Live in open beta with a retroactive PRD, a decision log, a competitor teardown, 1,265 SEO city pages and a beta scoped to one research question: do users trust the comparison enough to act on it? Monetisation is sequenced behind a fake-door test.

CASE 02 · LLM AGENT · EVALS

French Verb Tutor

A Telegram tutor that drills French conjugation across 8 tenses, decides what to practise next, and is checked by an eval suite before I trust its grading.

Problem
I am learning French and conjugation only sticks with daily, corrected practice. Apps drill what they choose. I wanted the verbs I actually get wrong to come back sooner, with the mistake explained in French.
Decisions that shaped it
  • Two modes on purpose. A fixed practice flow with spaced repetition, and a tool-calling agent that reads the student's history and chooses verbs and tenses itself.
  • Hand-rolled agent loop, no framework, so every model call and tool dispatch is visible. That was the point: learn what agents really do.
  • Cost guardrails by design. A hard ceiling on tool calls per message and a cap on conversation history, so a confused agent cannot drain the budget.
  • Provider-agnostic. Switching between OpenAI and Claude models is a one-line config change.
How I know the grader works
A 36-case eval set in 12 categories: correct answers, wrong endings, wrong tense, incomplete answers, non-answers, real student messages, and policy calls such as missing accents and elision. An LLM grader that marks a correct answer wrong destroys a learner's trust faster than any other bug.
Where it stands
Running daily on Railway with 85 verbs and a shareable verb list for my teacher. Next: a teacher mode to push specific verbs to students.

CASE 03 · VISION AI · AUTOMATION

Grocery Receipt Bot

Photograph a receipt in Telegram and every line item lands in a shared Google Sheet. A rebuild of a no-code workflow, with no new subscription.

Problem
My household tracked grocery spend through a no-code automation. It proved the habit, then its trial ended. The job was to keep the exact same behaviour for two users at zero added cost, without losing the data already collected.
Decisions that shaped it
  • Prototype first, build second. The no-code version validated the habit before any code was written.
  • Keep the existing sheet layout so history stays continuous. The bot only appends and never overwrites.
  • "Null, don't guess." The prompt tells the model to leave unreadable fields empty. A blank cell is recoverable, a confident wrong price is not.
  • Model fallback chain. Free-tier capacity was unreliable on the larger model, so requests try a lighter model first and fall back in order.
  • Designed failure states. "Couldn't read it", "no items found" and "parsed but couldn't save" are three different messages, because the user's next step differs.
What it leaves out
No database, no dashboard, no accounts. The spreadsheet is the product surface people already use. Access is an allowlist with an admin alert on unknown senders.
Where it stands
In daily use on French receipts in euros. Changes arrive as pull requests that I review before merge.

CASE 04 · UX REBUILD · PROTOTYPE

Mobile Store Locator Rebuild

A product owner case study and working prototype for a salon locator where about 90% of traffic is mobile.

Problem
Locator visitors are close to converting: they want to book, call or get directions. The existing flow showed a long list before the map, made the map hard to reach, filtered weakly and demanded a lot of scrolling before a useful result.
Decisions that shaped it
  • Map first on mobile, with distance on every pin so the choice can be made without opening a card.
  • Sticky filters for service, open now and distance, since those are the questions a visitor actually has.
  • Empty, loading and error states built in, including recovery from a search with no results.
  • Success defined before the build: time to identify a relevant store, CTA click-through on directions, call and booking, and scroll depth before the first useful result.
Why it matters
Store Locator is one of the components I own professionally (1M+ sessions). This rebuild shows how I frame a problem, set KPIs and test a direction in code before asking a team to commit.

03 — Approach

How I work.

The same habits show up at L'Oréal scale and in a weekend build.

01

Start from the user's job, not the feature list.

Every product names who it is for and what it deliberately leaves out. Saying no to a persona is a decision, and I write it down.

02

Prototype early.

A no-code or throwaway version first, to test the habit before investing in the build. Then a working version, because it answers questions a spec cannot.

03

Design the failure state.

AI features are wrong some of the time. I make the error visible and recoverable, and I would take an honest blank over a confident guess.

04

Measure before trusting.

Evals for model behaviour, A/B tests for user behaviour, and a success metric agreed before the work starts. Changes ship as reviewed pull requests with their test status.
